# Fertilizer timing around heavy rain and floods

## Why timing matters

Urea applied on the surface is easily lost. Heavy rain soon after application washes nitrogen out of the field through runoff and overflow, and flood water can carry it away completely. Lost fertilizer means lost money and poorer crops. Good timing and placement can save roughly a quarter to a third of nitrogen.

## Rules for top-dressing urea before rain

1. **Delay urea top-dress** if heavy rain (for example more than about 25–50 mm in a day) or a storm is forecast within the next **2 days**.
2. Apply urea when the weather will be calm for at least 2–3 days.
3. In rice, apply urea into **shallow standing water (about 2–3 cm) or on saturated soil**, then keep the field outlets closed for several days so water does not flow out.
4. In AWD fields, apply urea on moist soil just before re-flooding.
5. Never apply fertilizer on a field that is flooded above the plant or likely to overflow.

## Split application

Do not put all the nitrogen on at once. Typical splits for transplanted rice:

- **Basal:** all phosphorus, all or most potassium, and a small part of nitrogen at final land preparation.
- **First top-dress:** at early tillering, about 15–20 days after transplanting (DAT).
- **Second top-dress:** at active tillering, about 30–35 DAT.
- **Third top-dress:** at panicle initiation, about 45–50 DAT (depending on variety duration).

If rain prevents a split, apply it as soon as possible afterwards rather than doubling the next dose.

## Leaf Color Chart (LCC)

The IRRI Leaf Color Chart helps decide when nitrogen is needed.

1. Compare the uppermost fully opened leaf of 10 plants with the chart, shading the leaf with your body.
2. If 6 or more leaves are lighter than the critical value (usually **shade 4** for most transplanted inbred and hybrid rice, **shade 3** for light-green and aromatic varieties), **apply nitrogen**.
3. Check every 7–10 days from about 14 DAT until flowering.

## Urea deep placement (UDP)

Urea super granules (USG), or briquettes, are placed deep in the soil where rain and flood water cannot easily wash them away.

1. Use granules of about 1.8 g or 2.7 g.
2. **Place one granule** in the centre of every 4 hills, about **7–10 cm deep**, within about **7–10 days after transplanting**.
3. Transplant in straight lines (for example 20 x 20 cm) to make placement easy.
4. UDP is applied once for the whole season, replacing the urea top-dresses.
5. UDP has been promoted in Bangladesh by BRRI, DAE and IFDC and commonly saves about 25–35% of urea while maintaining or raising yields.

## After a flood

1. Wait until the water recedes and new leaves appear (about 7–10 days) before applying nitrogen.
2. **Apply** about 20–30 kg N/ha to help recovery; more is usually wasted.
3. Where the flood left fresh silt, reduce nitrogen; where it left sand, add some potassium.
4. Check with the LCC before further top-dressing.

## Other nutrients

- **Potassium** improves resistance to lodging and disease; apply it basal and at panicle initiation in sandy or flood-affected soils.
- **Zinc sulphate** (about 10 kg/ha) is useful on waterlogged and saline soils where zinc deficiency is common.
- **Phosphorus** is less easily washed out but can be lost with eroded soil; apply basal and incorporate.

## Vegetables and upland crops

- Apply fertilizer in bands or pockets and cover with soil.
- Use smaller, more frequent doses during the rainy season.
- Use organic manure to improve nutrient holding.
