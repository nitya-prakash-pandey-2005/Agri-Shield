# Jute production and flood management

## Jute and water

Jute is grown in Bangladesh and eastern India (West Bengal, Assam, Bihar and parts of Odisha) during the pre-monsoon and monsoon season. It is sown roughly from March to May and harvested from about July to September.

- **White jute** (Corchorus capsularis) tolerates waterlogging better and is grown on medium and low lands.
- **Tossa jute** (Corchorus olitorius) gives finer fibre but prefers well-drained medium to high land.
- **Young jute plants** (first 6–8 weeks) are sensitive to waterlogging and die if flooded for more than 1–2 days.
- **Older jute** (after about 2 months, when plants are tall) tolerates standing water around the stem base for several weeks, and can even form adventitious roots on the stem. It is not tolerant of complete submergence.

## Reducing flood risk

1. **Sow early** (March–April) on low land so plants are tall enough before the monsoon floods.
2. Grow tossa on higher land and white jute on lower land.
3. **Drain** fields in the early stage; make furrows every few metres leading to a drain.
4. Keep plant population at about 4–5 lakh plants per hectare (roughly 400,000–500,000) by thinning to about 7–10 cm between plants.
5. Use recommended fertilizer; avoid heavy nitrogen just before expected floods.

## Harvest timing when floods threaten

- The best time to harvest for fibre is at the **early pod or small pod stage**, typically about **100–120 days** after sowing.
- If a deep flood is forecast, **harvest early**. Jute harvested from about 90–100 days gives slightly less but still good-quality fibre. Losing the crop under deep water is far worse.
- Cut plants at ground level or pull them where the soil is very wet.
- Strip leaves in the field and bundle plants for retting.

## Retting

Retting frees the fibre from the stem using microbial action in water.

1. Use **clean, slow-moving or stagnant water** at least about 60–90 cm deep: ditches, ponds or flooded low land.
2. Keep bundles fully submerged using weights of logs or banana stems, but **do not use soil or mud**, which darkens the fibre.
3. Retting takes about **10–20 days** depending on water temperature.
4. Check daily near the end; fibre is ready when it slips easily from the stem.
5. Wash fibre in clean water and dry on bamboo racks in the sun, not on the ground.
6. Where water is scarce, use **ribbon retting**: strip the green bark ribbons and ret them in a small tank or pit, which needs much less water. Microbial retting accelerators developed by research institutes can shorten retting time.

## Seed crop protection

Flooding often destroys the seed crop, causing seed shortages for the next season.

1. Grow jute for seed on **high land** that does not flood.
2. **Late-sown seed crops**, sown around August–September after floods recede, are widely used in Bangladesh; they produce seed by about November–December.
3. Cut tops of fibre plants (top-cutting) and plant them on high land to produce seed where recommended by BJRI.
4. **Dry seed** to about 9–10% moisture and store in airtight containers above flood level.

## After a flood

- If young jute has been underwater more than 2–3 days, replace it with a flood-tolerant crop or transplant aman rice.
- In older jute, **drain** and harvest as soon as the soil allows walking.
- Use flood water bodies for retting.
