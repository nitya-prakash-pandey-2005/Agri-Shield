# Deciding when to harvest early before a flood or cyclone

## The basic trade-off

Harvesting a few days early loses a small amount of yield and quality. Losing the crop to flood water, lodging or a cyclone can mean losing everything. When a strong storm or flood is forecast within 2–3 days, early harvest is usually the better choice if the crop is close to maturity.

## Is the rice crop mature enough?

Rice is at harvest maturity when **about 80–85% of the grains are straw-coloured** (golden) and the grains in the lower part of the panicle are hard dough. This is usually about 28–35 days after flowering.

- **80–85% straw-coloured:** harvest now. Yield loss is negligible.
- **About 70–80% straw-coloured:** harvest if a severe flood or cyclone is likely. Expect a small yield loss (roughly 5–10%) and more immature grain.
- **Less than about 70%, grains still milky:** early harvest gives poor, chalky grain. Usually better to drain the field, protect the crop and accept the risk, unless complete loss is almost certain.

Grain moisture at harvest is typically about 20–25%.

## Other crops

- **Maize:** harvest when the black layer forms at the grain base. If flooding threatens earlier, harvest green cobs for sale as fresh maize.
- **Jute:** fibre can be harvested from about 100 days; harvest early if deep flood is forecast.
- **Vegetables:** pick all marketable produce; sell fresh or dry what can be dried.
- **Mungbean and groundnut:** harvest when most pods are mature; pull whole plants and dry under cover.

## Harvest steps before a storm

1. **Harvest** during dry hours; start with the most mature and lowest-lying fields.
2. Arrange combine harvesters or reapers early; they are in high demand before storms.
3. **Thresh** within 24 hours. Do not leave cut rice in wet heaps; it heats and discolours within 1–2 days.
4. Move harvested grain to a raised, covered place.

## Drying after early harvest

1. **Dry** grain to about **14% moisture** for storage as food grain and to **12% or below** for seed.
2. Sun-dry on tarpaulins or nets in layers of about 2–4 cm, turning every 30 minutes to 1 hour.
3. Avoid drying on bare road surfaces.
4. Where rain continues, use a flatbed or community dryer if available, or spread grain thinly indoors with fans.
5. Test dryness by biting: dry grain cracks cleanly. A cheap moisture meter is more reliable.

## Storage

**Store** dried grain in hermetic bags or sealed containers on raised platforms at least 30 cm above the highest expected flood level. See the seed and grain storage guidance.

## Records and support

- Note the harvest date, area and quantity harvested.
- Take photos of immature or damaged crop left in the field.
- Report to the local agriculture officer and your insurer; some schemes cover post-harvest losses from cyclones and unseasonal rain for crops lying cut in the field for a short period.
