# Community seedbeds, floating seedbeds and late transplanting

## Why keep seedlings in reserve

When a flood destroys newly transplanted rice, farmers need healthy seedlings within 1–2 weeks. Seedbeds on high ground or on water ensure seedlings are ready as soon as the water recedes.

## Types of emergency seedbeds

### Raised seedbeds
- Build beds 15–30 cm above the highest normal water level on high land, roadsides or homestead areas.
- Bed size about 1–1.25 m wide with drainage channels between beds.
- Sow about 40–60 g of seed per square metre for normal seedlings.

### Floating seedbeds (Bangladesh)
- Make a raft of banana stems or bamboo, cover with water hyacinth, then a thin layer of mud (2–3 cm).
- Sow pre-germinated seed on the mud. Tie the raft to a post so it rises with the water.

### Dapog seedbeds (Philippines and elsewhere)
- Spread pre-germinated seed thickly (about 1–1.5 kg per square metre) on banana leaves or plastic sheets on a flat surface, even on a house floor or a road.
- Keep moist by sprinkling. Seedlings are ready in about 9–14 days and are transplanted as mats.
- Useful when land is under water, but seedlings are small and need shallow water after transplanting.

## Community seedbed planning

1. Agree with the farmer group or local agriculture office on a shared seedbed site before the flood season.
2. Stagger sowing: sow a first batch at normal time and a second batch about 2–3 weeks later.
3. Use flood-tolerant (Sub1) or photoperiod-sensitive varieties that tolerate late planting.
4. Keep records of who contributes seed and labour and how seedlings are shared.

## Transplanting aged seedlings

After floods, seedlings in the reserve bed are often older than normal.

- Normal transplanting age: about 20–30 days.
- Seedlings up to about 40–50 days can still be used, especially photoperiod-sensitive aman varieties.
- For aged seedlings, **transplant** 3–4 seedlings per hill and use closer spacing (about 15 x 15 cm) to make up for fewer tillers.
- Trim leaf tips only if seedlings are very tall and will lodge; do not cut into the growing point.
- Apply a split of nitrogen (about 20–30 kg N/ha) 10–15 days after late transplanting.

## Tiller separation

If seedlings are short, separate 2–3 tillers from surviving hills in unflooded parts of the field and **transplant** them as new hills. Take tillers from hills about 30–40 days old.

## Choosing varieties for late planting

- **Photoperiod-sensitive varieties** (for example many traditional and some improved aman varieties in Bangladesh, and Swarna types in eastern India) flower at a fixed time of year, so late transplanting shortens their growth but they still mature before the dry season.
- **Short-duration varieties** of about 100–115 days are useful when the season is short. Ask the local agriculture office for recommended short-duration varieties for your area.
- If rice is no longer possible, plant mustard, lentil, grasspea, potato or vegetables as soon as the soil can be worked.
