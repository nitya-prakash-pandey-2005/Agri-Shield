# Farm cyclone and typhoon preparedness checklist

## Know the warnings

Cyclones and typhoons are tracked days in advance by national services: the Bangladesh Meteorological Department (BMD), India Meteorological Department (IMD), PAGASA in the Philippines, Vietnam's National Centre for Hydro-Meteorological Forecasting and BMKG in Indonesia. Follow official warnings through radio, TV, SMS and local officials. Act early; the last 24 hours are often too windy and wet for field work.

## 72 hours before landfall

1. **Check the forecast** track and strength; listen to local agriculture and disaster officials.
2. **Decide on early harvest.** Rice with about 80–85% straw-coloured grains, mature vegetables, fruits, and pulses should be harvested now.
3. Book harvesters and labour; arrange drying space.
4. **Clear drains** and field outlets; repair bunds.
5. Stop any planned fertilizer and pesticide applications; they will be washed away.
6. Check that livestock vaccinations are up to date and stock up on fodder.
7. Gather documents: land records, insurance policy, registration cards, bank details. Keep them in a waterproof bag.

## 48 hours before

1. **Harvest** and thresh as much as possible. Dry grain on raised surfaces or under cover.
2. **Store seed** and grain in hermetic bags or sealed containers on raised platforms or in a safe building.
3. **Lower water depth** in rice fields to about 2–3 cm so fields can absorb rain.
4. **Prune** dead or heavy branches of fruit trees near houses. Harvest mature coconuts and bunches of banana.
5. **Prop or tie** banana plants and young trees with bamboo stakes. Remove large leaves from banana plants if very strong winds are forecast to reduce wind load.
6. **Move equipment** — pumps, power tillers, sprayers, fertilizer and pesticide stocks — to high, secure places. Keep pesticides away from food and water.
7. Secure fish ponds: net the dikes and harvest marketable fish.

## 24 hours before

1. **Move livestock** to raised ground, killas, cyclone shelters with animal space, or strong sheds. Leave them untied if they cannot be moved, so they can escape rising water.
2. Store at least 3–5 days of dry fodder and clean drinking water for animals.
3. **Close sluice gates** and inlets where you are part of a polder or embankment system, following the water management group.
4. Tie down roofs of stores and animal sheds; cover grain stacks with plastic sheets.
5. Charge phones and keep a battery radio.
6. Follow evacuation orders. **People's safety comes first**; do not stay behind to guard crops or animals.

## After the cyclone passes

1. Wait for the official all-clear before going to fields.
2. Avoid fallen electric lines and damaged trees.
3. **Drain** fields and **flush** saline water if there was storm surge.
4. Take dated photos of damage and **report losses** to the local agriculture officer and insurer within the time limit (often 72 hours to about 10 days, depending on the scheme).
5. Check livestock for injury and disease; provide clean water.
6. Plan replanting with stored seed or community seedbeds.
