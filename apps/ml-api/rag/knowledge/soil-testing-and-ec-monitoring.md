# Soil sampling and EC monitoring for salinity

## Why monitor

Salinity in coastal soils changes month by month. It rises in the dry season as rivers carry less fresh water and evaporation pulls salt upward, and falls with monsoon rains. Regular measurement tells farmers when to plant, which variety to use, and whether to flush or leach.

## Taking a soil sample

1. Divide the farm into uniform areas (same soil, crop and water source). Sample each separately.
2. In each area, take **10–15 small samples (cores)** in a zigzag pattern, avoiding bunds, channels, manure heaps and field corners.
3. Sample the **topsoil at 0–15 cm**. For salinity, also take a separate sample at **15–30 cm**, as salts can move up or down.
4. Mix the cores from one area and depth well in a clean bucket; take about **500 g** as the composite sample.
5. Air-dry in the shade, crush clods and remove stones and roots.
6. Label with farmer name, field, depth and date.

For a full nutrient test, send the sample to a soil laboratory: SRDI laboratories and mobile labs in Bangladesh, soil testing labs under the Soil Health Card scheme in India, provincial or regional labs in Vietnam, the Philippines and Indonesia.

## Measuring EC with a handheld meter (1:5 method)

1. Calibrate the meter using its standard solution.
2. Weigh or measure **1 part dry soil** (for example 20 g, or 1 spoon) and **5 parts clean distilled or rain water** (100 mL, or 5 spoons).
3. Shake or stir well for a few minutes; let settle for about 30 minutes.
4. Dip the probe into the clear liquid and read the **EC 1:5** in dS/m (or mS/cm; they are equal).
5. Rinse the probe with clean water.

## Converting EC 1:5 to ECe

Crop thresholds are given as ECe (saturation extract). EC 1:5 readings must be multiplied by a factor that depends on soil texture. Published conversion factors are approximate:

| Soil texture | Approximate factor (ECe = EC1:5 x factor) |
|---|---|
| Heavy clay | about 6 |
| Clay, clay loam (typical paddy soils) | about 7–9 |
| Loam | about 9–10 |
| Sandy loam | about 10–11 |
| Sand and loamy sand | about 13–17 |

**Example:** a clay-loam paddy soil with EC 1:5 of 0.5 dS/m has an ECe of roughly 0.5 x 8 = 4 dS/m — moderately saline, above the rice threshold of 3 dS/m.

These factors are rough; for important decisions, confirm with a laboratory ECe test.

## Measuring water EC

- Measure EC of canal, river, pond and field water directly with the meter.
- Record the time and tide stage.
- For rice, irrigation water above about 2 dS/m reduces yield; above about 4 dS/m avoid using it at flowering.

## How often to measure

| Period | Frequency |
|---|---|
| Dry season (roughly January–May in BD and the Mekong) | Every **2 weeks** for soil; water before every irrigation |
| Before planting any crop | Once, 1–2 weeks before sowing or transplanting |
| After storm surge or saline flooding | Immediately, then every 2–4 weeks until ECe falls |
| Wet season | Monthly or at key stages |
| Full lab nutrient test | Every 2–3 years |

## Using the results

| ECe (dS/m) | Action |
|---|---|
| below 2 | Normal crops |
| 2–4 | **Flush**, use tolerant varieties for sensitive stages |
| 4–8 | Salt-tolerant rice or tolerant crops (barley, sunflower, cotton); leach before planting |
| above 8 | Leach, delay planting, or consider aquaculture or fallow |

## Keep a salinity log

Record date, field, depth, EC reading, converted ECe, water EC and tide. Comparing year to year shows trends and helps plan cropping calendars and sluice operation with neighbours.
