# Tidal flooding (rob) and salt intrusion on the Java north coast

## The problem

Along the north coast of Central Java (Pantura), including **Demak, Semarang, Pekalongan** and nearby areas, farmers face **rob**: tidal flooding of land during high tides. It is made worse by:

- **Land subsidence**, mainly from groundwater pumping and soft coastal soils. Some parts of Semarang, Demak and Pekalongan have been reported to sink by several centimetres per year, in places roughly 5–10 cm per year or more.
- **Coastal erosion** after mangroves were cleared for fish ponds.
- **Sea level rise** and high spring tides.

Some villages in Sayung (Demak) have lost large areas of rice fields and fish ponds to the sea. Rice fields near the coast suffer salt intrusion through canals and seepage, especially in the dry season.

## Know the risk periods

1. **Follow BMKG tidal flood (rob) warnings**, which highlight high spring tides around full and new moon.
2. Rob is often most severe during periods of very high tides, which occur several times a year; check the local tide calendar.
3. Use the **Kalender Tanam (KATAM) Terpadu** from Kementan to plan planting dates and varieties for your subdistrict.

## Field protection

1. **Raise and strengthen bunds** to about 30–50 cm above normal tide water levels on the seaward side of fields; compact soil and plant grass on bunds.
2. Install simple **one-way (flap) gates** on drainage outlets so water can drain at low tide but sea water cannot flow back at high tide.
3. **Close inlets** when canal water is saline; **measure** EC before letting water in. For rice, use water below about 2 dS/m.
4. **Drain** fields at low tide after tidal flooding and **flush** with rain or fresh irrigation water.
5. Work with the farmers' water user association (P3A) to coordinate gate operation across the block.

## Suitable varieties

- **Inpari 34 Salin Agritan** and **Inpari 35 Salin Agritan**: released as salt-tolerant irrigated rice varieties.
- **Inpara** varieties: developed for swamp and tidal lowlands, tolerating some waterlogging, iron toxicity and salinity.
- **Inpari 30 Ciherang Sub1** and **Inpari 29 Rendaman**: for fields where short-term submergence is the main risk.

Ask the local BPP (agricultural extension centre) or BPTP (assessment institute) which varieties are available.

## Cropping calendar adjustments

1. Plant the main rice crop in the rainy season (MT1, roughly November–March) when fresh water is available and salinity is lowest.
2. In the second season (MT2), plant only where fresh water is reliable; otherwise choose salt-tolerant varieties or shorter-duration crops.
3. Avoid having rice at flowering during the driest months when salt intrusion peaks.

## Mangrove belts and coastal protection

- **Mangrove belts** of at least roughly 100 m width between the sea and farmland reduce wave energy, trap sediment and slow erosion.
- In Demak, the Building with Nature approach used **permeable bamboo and brushwood structures** to trap sediment so mangroves can re-establish.
- Farmers can join community mangrove planting and protect young mangroves from cutting and grazing.
- **Silvofishery** (fish or shrimp ponds combined with mangroves) is an option where rice is no longer viable.

## When rice is no longer possible

Where land is permanently flooded or highly saline:
1. Shift to **brackish aquaculture** (milkfish, tilapia, shrimp) with mangrove protection.
2. Grow vegetables on raised surjan beds with fresh rain water.
3. Seek advice from the agriculture and fisheries offices on transition support.

## Documentation

Record dates and depth of rob flooding and salt damage with photos, and report crop losses to the PPL for AUTP insurance claims (damage of about 75% or more is typically required).
