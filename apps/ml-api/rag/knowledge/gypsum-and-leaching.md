# Gypsum and leaching for saline and sodic soils

## Saline versus sodic soil

- **Saline soil:** too much soluble salt (ECe above about 4 dS/m), pH usually below 8.5, soil structure normal. Treatment: **leach** the salt out with fresh water.
- **Sodic soil:** too much sodium on the clay (exchangeable sodium percentage, ESP, above about 15), pH often above 8.5, soil hard and crusted when dry, sticky and slow to drain when wet. Treatment: **apply gypsum** to replace sodium with calcium, then leach.
- **Saline–sodic soil:** both problems. Apply gypsum and leach together; leaching alone can make the soil sodic and hard.

Coastal soils flooded by sea water are mainly saline, but repeated surges can raise sodium too. A soil test for EC, pH and ideally ESP tells you which problem you have.

## Applying gypsum

1. Get a soil test. Gypsum requirement is calculated from ESP and soil texture.
2. Typical rates on sodic soils are about **2–5 t/ha**, with higher rates for severe sodicity. Follow the rate from the soil lab or local agriculture office.
3. Use finely ground agricultural gypsum (calcium sulphate).
4. **Broadcast** gypsum evenly on dry soil and mix into the top 10–15 cm by ploughing or harrowing.
5. Immediately pond fresh water (about 5–10 cm) for several days so the calcium reacts and the displaced sodium is washed down.
6. **Drain** the water off, then pond and drain again 2–3 times if fresh water is available.
7. Add organic matter such as farmyard manure or compost (about 5–10 t/ha) or green manure (dhaincha) to improve structure.

After storm-surge flooding, smaller gypsum doses are sometimes advised where sodium has risen; base the rate on a soil test.

## Leaching salts

Leaching means passing enough fresh water through the root zone to carry salt below it.

- **Rule of thumb:** ponding and draining about 30 cm of fresh water removes a large part (very roughly 70–80%) of the salt from the top 30 cm of soil, if drainage works. Intermittent ponding (several smaller applications) uses less water than continuous ponding.
- Leaching only works if the water can **drain away**. Clear field drains and outlets first.

### Steps for leaching a rice field before planting
1. Plough or puddle lightly to break the surface crust.
2. **Flush** the field: fill with fresh rain or canal water (EC below about 1–2 dS/m) to 5–10 cm.
3. Leave for 1–2 days, then **drain** the water out through the outlet (not into a neighbour's field).
4. Repeat 2–3 times.
5. **Measure** EC of the drained water and the soil; transplant when soil ECe is below about 4 dS/m for tolerant varieties, or below about 3 dS/m for ordinary rice.

## Leaching requirement

The leaching requirement is the extra fraction of irrigation water needed to keep salts from building up. FAO 29 gives an approximate formula:

LR = ECw / (5 x ECe_target - ECw)

Example: irrigating rice with water of ECw 1.5 dS/m and keeping ECe at 3 dS/m needs LR = 1.5 / (15 - 1.5), about 0.11, meaning roughly 11% extra water should drain below the root zone.

## Practical tips

- Use the first heavy monsoon rains to leach; delay transplanting until 2–3 flushes have been done.
- Do not leave saline fields bare and dry in the hot season; mulching with straw (about 3–5 t/ha) reduces salt rising to the surface.
- **Apply** potassium and zinc on saline soils; sodium reduces their uptake.
