# Preparing field drainage before heavy rain

## Why drain before the rain

A field that is already full of water cannot store more rain. If standing water is lowered before a storm, the field can absorb 5–10 cm of extra rain before plants are fully submerged. Most rice varieties are harmed when water covers the whole plant for more than a few days, and upland crops such as vegetables, maize and mungbean can die after only 1–2 days of waterlogging.

## Timing after a forecast

Act as soon as a heavy rain, depression or typhoon forecast is issued, ideally **24–72 hours before** the rain arrives.

- **72 hours before:** inspect outlets, channels and bunds; organise labour and tools.
- **48 hours before:** clear drains and lower water depth in rice fields.
- **24 hours before:** final check of outlets; move pumps, fertilizer and seed to high ground.

## Step-by-step drainage preparation

1. **Clear outlets.** Remove weeds, silt, water hyacinth and rubbish from field outlets, culverts and the main drain. A blocked outlet is the most common reason fields stay flooded.
2. **Lower water depth.** In transplanted rice, drain down to about 2–3 cm of standing water, or to saturated soil if the crop is well established. Do not leave young seedlings (under about 10 days after transplanting) completely dry in hot sun.
3. **Repair bunds.** Fill rat holes and crab holes, rebuild low spots and compact the bund. A bund about 30 cm high and 30–40 cm wide at the top holds well in most paddies.
4. **Make an overflow point.** Cut a controlled spillway at the lowest safe point so excess water leaves without breaking the bund.
5. **Dig cross drains** in vegetable and upland fields: shallow furrows about 20–30 cm deep every few metres leading to the field drain.
6. **Coordinate with neighbours.** Drainage works only if downstream channels are clear. Agree with neighbouring farmers or the water user group to clear shared drains together.
7. **Close sluice gates** or inlets that could let river or tidal water in during the storm, where you are part of a polder or embankment system.

## Special cases

- **Near harvest:** if rice is more than about 80–85% mature, consider harvesting early instead of relying on drainage.
- **Just fertilized fields:** do not top-dress urea within about 2 days before heavy rain; it will be washed away.
- **Raised beds and sorjan:** make sure the sunken channels connect to an outlet so water does not back up into beds.
- **Sugarcane and jute:** open furrow ends so water runs off quickly.

## After the rain

1. Open outlets fully as soon as rain stops to remove water within 1–3 days.
2. Check bunds again for breaches.
3. Record flood depth and duration; this helps decisions on replanting and insurance claims.

## Quick checklist

- [ ] Outlets and culverts cleared
- [ ] Water lowered to 2–3 cm in rice
- [ ] Bunds repaired and holes filled
- [ ] Spillway cut at a safe point
- [ ] Inlets and sluice gates closed if river or tide is rising
- [ ] Pumps, seed and fertilizer moved to high ground
