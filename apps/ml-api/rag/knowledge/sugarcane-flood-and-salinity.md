# Sugarcane under flooding, waterlogging and salinity

## Salinity

Sugarcane is **salt-sensitive**. Yield starts to fall at a soil ECe of about **1.7 dS/m** and drops roughly **6% for every 1 dS/m** above that. At about ECe 5 dS/m, expect roughly 20% yield loss; at about 10 dS/m, roughly half the yield is lost. Young cane and germinating setts are most sensitive.

### Managing salinity
1. **Measure** soil and irrigation water EC before planting.
2. Avoid planting sugarcane where ECe is above about 4 dS/m unless you can leach salts.
3. Use fresh irrigation water (ECw below about 1.1 dS/m for full yield).
4. Plant setts in **furrows** and irrigate in furrows; salts accumulate on ridges, away from the setts.
5. **Apply** organic manure (about 10–25 t/ha) or press mud to improve soil structure.
6. On sodic soils, **apply gypsum** according to soil test before planting.
7. Mulch with cane trash (about 5–10 t/ha) to reduce surface evaporation and salt rise.

## Flooding and waterlogging

Established sugarcane tolerates waterlogging better than most upland crops. Mature cane can survive standing water for several weeks, though growth slows and sugar content falls. It is much less tolerant when young or completely submerged.

- **Germination and early growth (first 2–3 months):** very sensitive; setts rot within a few days in waterlogged soil.
- **Grand growth stage:** tolerates partial flooding for 2–4 weeks with some yield and quality loss; plants form aerial roots.
- **Complete submergence** of the cane top for more than a few days kills the growing point.

### Before the flood season
1. **Drain** fields: dig field drains about 45–60 cm deep, with outlets to the main drain.
2. **Earth up** the crop at about 90–120 days after planting to strengthen the base and reduce lodging.
3. Complete nitrogen application before the monsoon peak.
4. Use varieties recommended for waterlogged areas where available.

### After the flood
1. **Drain** standing water as fast as possible; aim to remove it within 3–4 days.
2. **Prop lodged cane**: tie 2–3 clumps together using dry leaves (trash twisting) or support with bamboo, especially after strong winds.
3. Remove dead and rotting leaves; remove aerial roots only if recommended locally.
4. **Apply** an extra nitrogen dose of about 25–30 kg N/ha when the soil drains, to help recovery.
5. Watch for red rot and wilt diseases, which spread in waterlogged fields; remove and burn infected clumps.
6. If a flooded crop is near maturity and sugar recovery is falling, arrange **early harvest** with the sugar mill.

## Cyclones and strong winds

1. **Tie** cane clumps together (propping) at about 150 and 210 days after planting in cyclone-prone areas.
2. Earthing up and trash twisting reduce lodging.
3. Lodged cane can still be harvested, but harvest it soon because it may sprout and lose sugar.

## Ratoon crops after floods

- Stubble that was flooded for a long time often gives a poor ratoon; check sprouting 3–4 weeks after harvest.
- Gap-fill with pre-sprouted setts or replant if more than about 30% of the stubble is dead.
