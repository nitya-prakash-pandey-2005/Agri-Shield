# Rice farming in flood-prone Pampanga and Central Luzon

## The flood setting

Pampanga lies in the lower Pampanga River Basin, including the Candaba swamp, and receives water from Nueva Ecija, Bulacan and Tarlac. Fields flood from heavy monsoon (habagat) rain, typhoons and river overflow. The **typhoon season runs roughly June to November**, with the greatest flood risk usually from **August to October**. Some low-lying areas stay flooded for weeks.

Farmers commonly plant:
- **Wet season crop:** about June–July sowing, harvest September–November — highest flood risk.
- **Dry season crop:** about November–January sowing, harvest March–May — usually the more reliable, higher-yielding crop. In parts of Candaba, only the dry season crop is possible.

## Choosing varieties

1. In fields that flood for up to about 2 weeks, plant **NSIC Rc 194 (Submarino 1)**, a submergence-tolerant variety that can survive roughly 14–17 days under water.
2. Use **early-maturing varieties** (about 105–115 days) in the wet season so the crop is harvested before the peak typhoon months. Ask the Municipal Agriculturist Office (MAO) or PhilRice for recommended early-maturing inbreds and hybrids for your area.
3. Use certified seed from registered seed growers or PhilRice seed programmes.

## Synchronous planting

1. **Plant within the same 2–3 week window** as neighbours in the same irrigation service area. This reduces pest build-up (tungro, stemborer, rats) and allows the whole block to be harvested before typhoons.
2. Follow the cropping calendar set by the irrigators' association and NIA.
3. Late plantings are more likely to be caught by October floods.

## Using PAGASA warnings

- **Tropical Cyclone Wind Signals (1 to 5):** under Signal 1–2, start early harvest of mature crops and secure seed and equipment; under Signal 3 and above, move people and livestock to safety.
- **Heavy rainfall warnings (yellow, orange, red):** yellow means flooding possible in low-lying areas; orange means flooding is threatening; red means serious flooding is expected — evacuate if told.
- **Flood bulletins** for the Pampanga River Basin give river level forecasts.

## Before a typhoon or flood

1. **Harvest** rice that is 80–85% mature.
2. **Drain** fields and clear canals and farm ditches.
3. **Store** seed and palay in sealed bags on raised platforms or the second floor.
4. Move carabao, cattle, goats and pigs to high ground.
5. Postpone fertilizer application.
6. Make sure your crop is insured with PCIC and your RSBSA registration is up to date.

## After the flood

1. **Drain gradually** and remove debris.
2. Check survival after about 5–7 days. If fewer than about half of hills survive, replant using dapog or wet-bed seedlings of an early-maturing variety.
3. **Top-dress** about 20–30 kg N/ha (about 1 bag of urea per hectare is roughly 23 kg N) 7–10 days after the water recedes when new leaves appear.
4. Watch for bacterial leaf blight, sheath blight and golden apple snail; handpick snails and eggs and keep water shallow for the first 2 weeks after replanting.
5. **File a Notice of Loss** with PCIC through your MAO or PCIC office promptly (within about 10 days of the loss).

## Diversification

- Grow vegetables (eggplant, okra, ampalaya, string beans) on raised beds or sorjan in the dry season.
- Integrate ducks or fish in some fields; ducks help control snails and weeds.
