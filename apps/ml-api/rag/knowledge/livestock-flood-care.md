# Livestock and poultry care during floods and cyclones

## Before the flood season

1. **Vaccinate** cattle and buffalo against **haemorrhagic septicaemia (HS)** and **foot-and-mouth disease (FMD)** before the monsoon; HS outbreaks often follow floods. Also vaccinate against black quarter and anthrax in areas where these occur.
2. **Deworm** animals before the rains.
3. Vaccinate poultry and ducks against Newcastle disease and duck plague as advised by the livestock office.
4. **Store fodder** on raised platforms or rooftops: dry rice straw, hay and, where possible, silage in sealed plastic bags. Plan for at least 2–3 weeks of feed.
5. Identify the nearest **raised ground**: a killa (raised earth mound for animals in Bangladesh), embankment, school ground or cyclone shelter with space for animals.
6. Raise the floor of sheds and poultry houses above normal flood level where possible.

## When a flood or cyclone warning is issued

1. **Move animals to high ground** or a killa 24–48 hours before the expected flood.
2. Take fodder, a water container, ropes and any medicines.
3. If animals cannot be moved, **untie them** so they can swim or escape. Tied animals drown.
4. Move poultry and small animals into raised cages or onto platforms.
5. Keep animals away from electric lines and weak structures during cyclones.

## During the flood

- **Clean drinking water:** give animals clean water, not flood water. Flood water carries disease, dead animals and chemicals. Adult cattle need roughly 30–50 litres of water per day, more in hot weather.
- **Feeding:** feed dry straw and concentrates; if fodder runs short, use tree leaves (jackfruit, banana leaves and stems) and water hyacinth in small amounts as emergency feed. Avoid mouldy fodder.
- Keep animals dry and shaded; avoid overcrowding on small mounds.
- Watch for signs of disease: high fever, swelling of the throat (HS), mouth or foot lesions (FMD), diarrhoea.

## After the water recedes

1. Clean and disinfect sheds with lime or a recommended disinfectant before returning animals.
2. **Remove and bury dead animals** away from water sources, at least 1 m deep with lime.
3. **Deworm** animals 2–3 weeks after the flood; liver fluke and roundworm increase after grazing on flooded land.
4. Arrange **booster vaccination** for HS and FMD through the livestock office if the flood coincided with the vaccination schedule or outbreaks are reported.
5. Avoid grazing on fields with thick silt or stagnant water for a few days.
6. Report deaths and disease outbreaks to the upazila or block livestock officer; this is needed for compensation and outbreak control.

## Ducks as a flood adaptation

Ducks thrive in flood-prone and wetland areas:
- They feed on snails, insects and aquatic weeds, reducing feed costs.
- Breeds such as Khaki Campbell and local breeds produce well under village conditions.
- Rice–duck systems help control golden apple snails and weeds in rice fields.
- Keep ducks in raised night houses and vaccinate against duck plague and duck cholera.

## Fodder security tips

- Grow fodder grasses (Napier, para grass) on embankments and high ground.
- Store straw bundles under a roof on bamboo platforms.
- Make urea-treated straw only with correct guidance and when water is available, to improve feed value.
