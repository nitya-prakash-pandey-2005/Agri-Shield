# Rice crop recovery after flood water recedes

## First 48 hours after the water goes down

The first two days decide how much of the crop can be saved.

1. **Drain gradually.** Open outlets step by step so water falls over 1–2 days. Fast drainage followed by strong sun can scorch leaves that have been under water.
2. **Remove debris.** Clear floating water hyacinth, straw and rubbish that lie on the plants.
3. **Wash off silt.** Where a thin silt layer covers leaves, gently shake plants or splash with clean water so leaves can photosynthesise.
4. **Keep 2–3 cm of water** after the flood if possible, instead of leaving the field completely dry, to help root recovery.
5. **Repair bunds** and clear field channels so the next rain does not re-flood the field.

## Check crop survival (days 3–7)

Wait about 5–7 days before deciding to replant. Plants that look dead often produce new leaves from the base.

- Count surviving hills in 3–5 spots of 1 square metre each.
- If more than about 70% of hills survive, keep the crop and gap-fill.
- If less than about 50% survive, consider re-transplanting the whole field or switching to a short-duration variety.
- Sub1 varieties recover well after up to about 14–17 days of submergence. Normal varieties rarely recover after more than about 7 days.

## Gap-filling and re-transplanting

1. Collect seedlings from community seedbeds, neighbours, or a reserve seedbed on high ground.
2. Use older seedlings (up to about 40–50 days) of Sub1 or photoperiod-sensitive varieties if planting is late.
3. **Transplant** 3–4 seedlings per hill for late planting to compensate for fewer tillers.
4. Splitting tillers from surviving hills (2–3 tillers per new hill) is a good source when seedlings are scarce.
5. If it is too late for transplanting, **direct-seed** a short-duration variety, or plant a non-rice crop such as mustard, lentil or vegetables at the end of the season.

## Fertilizer after the flood

- **Top-dress 20–30 kg N/ha** (about 45–65 kg urea/ha) about 7–10 days after the water recedes, when new green leaves appear.
- Do not apply nitrogen while water is still standing on the field.
- Where the flood left sandy deposits, apply potassium (for example about 20–30 kg K2O/ha) as well.
- Where fertile silt was deposited, reduce the next nitrogen dose.

## Disease and pest watch (2–4 weeks after flood)

Damaged leaves and wet conditions favour disease. Walk the field every 3–4 days.

- **Bacterial leaf blight:** yellow to straw-coloured stripes from leaf tips and edges, spreads with flood water. Drain the field for a few days, avoid extra nitrogen, and apply potash.
- **Brown spot:** oval brown spots, common on nutrient-poor or stressed plants. Correct nutrient deficiencies.
- **Sheath blight:** grey-green lesions on sheaths near the water line in dense crops.
- **Rats and snails:** rats move from flooded areas into surviving crops; golden apple snails spread with flood water in the Philippines and Vietnam.

## Record the damage

Take dated photos of the flooded field and the damage, note the dates of flooding, and **report the loss** to your local agriculture officer and crop insurer within the time limit of your scheme (often 72 hours to about 10 days). Keep seed and input receipts for replanting support.
