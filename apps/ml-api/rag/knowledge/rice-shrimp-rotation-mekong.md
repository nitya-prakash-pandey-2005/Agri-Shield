# Rice–shrimp rotation in the Mekong Delta

## The system

In coastal provinces such as Ca Mau, Bac Lieu, Soc Trang and Kien Giang, many farmers grow **brackish-water shrimp in the dry season** (roughly January to July) when canal water is saline, and **one rice crop in the rainy season** (roughly August to December) when rain and river water are fresh. The same field is used for both. Similar systems exist in parts of south-west Bangladesh.

The field has a central platform for rice surrounded by a **trench** (about 1–1.5 m deep and several metres wide) that holds water and shelters shrimp. Shrimp and sometimes crabs or fish may stay in the trench during the rice crop.

## Why it works

- Rice uses the nutrients left from the shrimp season.
- Shrimp use the rice stubble and organisms that grow after the rice harvest.
- Farmers earn from both crops, and the system spreads risk.
- It is a practical adaptation where salinity makes two rice crops impossible.

## Getting salt out before rice

The key step is to remove enough salt so rice seedlings survive.

1. At the end of the shrimp season, harvest shrimp and **drain** the platform.
2. Wait for the first good rains (usually July–August).
3. **Flush** the field: let rain water collect on the platform, then drain it out. Repeat 2–3 times, or more in dry years.
4. Pump out the saline water from the trench where possible and refill with rain water.
5. **Measure** soil and water salinity. Transplant when field water is below about 2–3 g/L (roughly 3–5 dS/m) for salt-tolerant varieties, and lower for ordinary varieties.
6. Remove excess sludge from the trench and use it on bunds, not on the rice platform.

## Rice crop management

1. **Use salt-tolerant varieties** adapted to rice–shrimp fields. Aromatic ST-series varieties from Soc Trang (for example ST24 and ST25) and some OM-series and traditional varieties such as Mot Bui Do are widely grown. Check with local extension staff.
2. **Transplant** older seedlings (about 25–35 days) rather than broadcasting, because older seedlings tolerate residual salinity better.
3. Keep the rice field flooded with fresh water throughout the season; avoid letting it dry out, because drying concentrates salts.
4. Use modest fertilizer rates; the soil is often fertile after shrimp. Avoid pesticides that are toxic to shrimp and crabs.
5. **Harvest** rice by about December or January before salinity rises again.

## Shrimp season management

1. After rice harvest, let saline water in gradually through the sluice.
2. Stock shrimp post-larvae only after checking salinity and water quality.
3. Maintain trench depth and remove sludge.
4. Avoid over-stocking; extensive and improved-extensive systems suit rice–shrimp fields better than intensive ponds.

## Risks to watch

- **Dry years:** if rains are late or weak, salt is not flushed and rice fails. Delay transplanting or skip rice rather than lose seed and fertilizer.
- **Early salinity intrusion:** harvest rice early if forecasts show early salinity.
- **Disease in shrimp:** poor water quality after heavy rain causes stress; manage pH and salinity changes.
