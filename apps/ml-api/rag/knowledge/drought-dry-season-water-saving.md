# Dry-season drought and water saving for smallholders

## When drought hits

Drought risk is highest in the dry season and during late or weak monsoons, especially in El Nino years. Rice is most damaged by water shortage at **flowering**, when even a few days of stress cause empty grains. Upland crops suffer most at germination and flowering.

## Choosing crops and varieties

1. **Grow short-duration varieties** (about 100–115 days for rice) so the crop finishes before water runs out.
2. **Drought-tolerant rice varieties:**
   - India: Sahbhagi Dhan (about 105–110 days), and DRR Dhan 42 and DRR Dhan 44 in some states.
   - Bangladesh: BRRI dhan 56 and BRRI dhan 57 (short-duration aman varieties that escape terminal drought), and BRRI dhan 71.
   - Philippines: Sahod Ulan varieties released through PhilRice and NSIC.
   - Indonesia: Inpago upland varieties and rainfed-lowland varieties advised by Balitbangtan.
3. **Replace dry-season rice with less thirsty crops** where water is short: mungbean, lentil, grasspea, maize, wheat, sunflower, mustard and vegetables. A rice crop needs roughly 1,000–1,500 mm of water; maize and pulses need far less.

## Saving water in rice

- **Alternate wetting and drying (AWD):** irrigate when water in the field tube drops to 15 cm below the surface; saves about 15–30% of water. Keep flooded around flowering.
- **Direct-seeded rice (DSR):** sowing dry or wet seed directly in the field instead of transplanting saves water for the nursery and puddling, and often saves labour.
  1. Use about **20–40 kg/ha** of seed for drill or line sowing (higher for broadcasting).
  2. Level the field well, ideally with laser or careful manual levelling.
  3. Control weeds early (pre-emergence herbicide or weeding at about 15–20 and 30–35 days after sowing).
- **Levelling:** a well-levelled field needs less water to cover.
- **Repair bunds** and plug leaks to reduce seepage.
- Use **plastic or lined field channels** where losses are high.

## Mulching and soil moisture

1. **Mulch** upland crops with rice straw at about **3–5 t/ha** (a layer of roughly 5 cm) to reduce evaporation and keep soil cooler.
2. **Relay-sow** pulses (lentil, grasspea, mungbean) into standing rice about 1–2 weeks before rice harvest to use residual moisture.
3. Use **zero tillage** for wheat, maize and pulses after rice to save soil moisture and time.
4. Add compost or manure to improve water holding.

## Irrigation scheduling

1. Irrigate at **critical stages**: for rice, panicle initiation and flowering; for wheat, crown root initiation (about 20–25 days after sowing) and flowering; for maize, tasselling and silking.
2. Irrigate early morning or evening to reduce evaporation.
3. Use **furrow or alternate-furrow irrigation** for upland crops instead of flooding.
4. Use **drip or pitcher irrigation** for vegetables and fruit seedlings.

## Water harvesting

1. **Store rainwater** in farm ponds, re-excavated canals and ditches before the dry season.
2. Deepen ponds and plant grass on banks to reduce erosion.
3. Share pump schedules with neighbours to reduce conveyance losses.

## When drought is severe

- Prioritise water for crops closest to harvest.
- Cut and feed drought-damaged green crops to livestock rather than letting them dry out.
- **Report crop losses** to the extension office and insurer; drought is covered by PMFBY, PCIC, AUTP and some weather-index schemes.
