# Farming in Bangladesh coastal polders

## What polders are

Polders are areas of land enclosed by earthen embankments, with sluice gates to control water. Most of Bangladesh's roughly 139 coastal polders were built by the Bangladesh Water Development Board from the 1960s onwards under the Coastal Embankment Project, to keep out tides and salt water and allow rice farming. Many are in Satkhira, Khulna, Bagerhat, Barguna and Patuakhali.

## Common problems

- **Embankment breaches** during cyclones and tidal surges. After **Cyclone Aila (May 2009)**, breaches in polders of Shyamnagar, Assasuni (Satkhira), Koyra and Dacope (Khulna) left land under tidal water for up to about two years. Soils stayed saline for several seasons.
- **Waterlogging** inside polders when rivers silt up and drainage through sluices fails.
- **Dry-season salinity** in soil and river water. Salinity rises from around January and **peaks around March–May**, the boro and rabi season.
- Conflict between rice farming and brackish shrimp (bagda) farming over saline water.

## Seasonal calendar and salinity

| Season | Months (approx.) | Salinity | Main crops |
|---|---|---|---|
| Aman | July–November | Low (monsoon) | Transplanted aman rice |
| Rabi / boro | December–May | Rising to peak March–May | Boro rice where fresh water exists, rabi crops |
| Aus / pre-monsoon | April–July | Falling with rain | Aus rice, vegetables |

## Rice options

1. **Aman:** salt-tolerant and submergence-tolerant varieties such as BRRI dhan 53, 54, 73 and 78, and Sub1 varieties in low patches.
2. **Boro:** salt-tolerant varieties such as BRRI dhan 47, 67, 97, 99 and BINA dhan 8 and 10, only where fresh water from ponds, canals or rivers remains below about 2–4 dS/m until harvest.
3. **Transplant** boro early so the crop flowers before peak salinity.

## Dry-season non-rice crops (BARI recommendations)

Where fresh water is limited or soil is moderately saline after aman harvest:
- **Sunflower:** moderately salt tolerant; sow in December–January after aman.
- **Watermelon:** very profitable in Khulna, Barguna and Patuakhali; grow on raised beds or pits with fresh pond water.
- **Barley:** among the most salt-tolerant cereals (threshold ECe about 8 dS/m).
- **Wheat** (threshold about 6 dS/m), **mungbean**, **chilli**, **maize** and **vegetables** on raised beds or sorjan where salinity is low.
- **Mulch** with rice straw after sowing to reduce evaporation and salt rise.

Sow rabi crops as early as possible after aman harvest, with zero or minimum tillage, to use residual moisture before salinity rises.

## Water management in the polder

1. **Store fresh water** in canals, khals and ponds at the end of the monsoon; re-excavate silted canals.
2. **Close sluice gates at high tide** in the dry season; open at low tide only to drain or when river water is fresh.
3. Join the local **Water Management Group** formed under BWDB participatory water management; programmes such as Blue Gold helped these groups plan gate operation and canal maintenance.
4. Report embankment weak points and cracks to BWDB before the cyclone season (April–May and October–November).

## After a breach or surge

1. Help close the breach; without it, fields cannot be drained.
2. **Drain** saline water at low tide through gates.
3. **Flush** fields with rain water and **measure** soil EC before planting.
4. Grow salt-tolerant crops first; add organic matter; **apply gypsum** where soil tests show sodium problems.
