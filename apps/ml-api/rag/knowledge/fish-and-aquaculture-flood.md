# Protecting fish ponds and aquaculture from floods

## Main risks

Floods overtop pond dikes and wash away fish, bring in predatory and wild fish, lower water quality, and can break dikes. Cyclones add strong winds, storm surge and sudden salinity changes. A single overtopping can lose most of a season's stock.

## Before the flood season

1. **Raise dikes** to at least about **30–60 cm above the highest flood level** remembered locally.
2. Make dikes wide and compact them; plant grass or vetiver on dike slopes to prevent erosion.
3. Repair holes made by crabs, rats and snakes.
4. Install **outlet pipes with screens** so excess water can leave without fish escaping.
5. Keep **nets and bamboo poles** ready to fence the pond.
6. Plan the stocking calendar so that fish reach market size before the peak flood months.

## When a flood or heavy rain is forecast

1. **Harvest** fish that are already marketable. Partial harvest reduces the loss if the pond overtops.
2. **Install net fencing** around the pond on bamboo poles, about **1–1.5 m above the dike**, with the bottom edge buried or pegged into the dike.
3. Lower the pond water level a little to make room for rain water, if the outlet allows.
4. Reduce or stop feeding 1–2 days before heavy rain; uneaten feed decays and reduces oxygen.
5. Store feed, lime and equipment on high ground.
6. For cages in rivers, move them to sheltered places and strengthen moorings.

## During and after the flood

1. Check dikes and nets daily; repair breaches quickly.
2. Watch for fish gasping at the surface in the early morning, a sign of low oxygen. Add fresh water, use aerators if available, or splash the water surface.
3. After the water recedes, **remove wild and predatory fish** that entered the pond by netting.
4. **Apply lime** (agricultural lime, about **250 kg/ha**, roughly 1 kg per decimal) to correct water acidity and disinfect after floods; adjust to local advice.
5. Refill carefully and wait a few days for water quality to stabilise before feeding normally.
6. Check fish for wounds and disease (for example red spot or ulcer disease), which often appear after floods and cold, cloudy weather.

## Stocking after floods

- **Avoid stocking during the peak flood period.**
- Restock with **larger fingerlings** (for example about 10–15 cm) after the flood season so they grow fast and resist disease.
- Use fast-growing species such as tilapia, pangas or carps according to local markets and advice.

## Adaptation options

- **Cage and pen culture** in floodplains uses flood water instead of fighting it.
- **Rice–fish culture** in seasonally flooded fields adds income without new ponds.
- **Community-based floodplain fisheries** stock fingerlings in seasonally flooded areas and share the harvest.
- In coastal areas, choose species that tolerate salinity changes (for example tilapia tolerates some brackish water; tiger shrimp and mud crab for brackish ponds).

## Shrimp and brackish ponds

1. Strengthen dikes before cyclone season.
2. Monitor salinity after heavy rain; sudden drops stress shrimp. Keep water deeper (at least about 80–100 cm) to buffer changes.
3. **Harvest** shrimp early if a cyclone or surge is forecast.

## Records

Record stocking date, number and size of fish stocked, and feed purchased. Take photos of damage for fisheries department support or insurance.
