# Mekong Delta drought and salinity intrusion 2016 and 2020 — lessons for farmers

## What happened in 2015–2016

The strong El Nino of 2015–2016 brought low rainfall and very low Mekong River flows in the dry season. Sea water moved much further inland than usual along the river branches and canals. The event was widely reported as the most severe drought and salinity intrusion in about 90 years of records. Saline water reached roughly 70–90 km inland on some river branches.

- Large areas of winter–spring rice were damaged, reported at well over 100,000 ha across the delta, together with fruit orchards and aquaculture.
- Many households also faced shortages of fresh drinking water.
- Several provinces, including Ben Tre, Tra Vinh, Soc Trang, Bac Lieu, Ca Mau and Kien Giang, were among the worst affected.

## What happened in 2019–2020

Salinity intrusion in the 2019–2020 dry season started earlier and in some places reached further inland than in 2016. Several provinces declared emergencies. However, **rice losses were much smaller than in 2016**, mainly because:

1. Authorities issued salinity forecasts months ahead.
2. Farmers **sowed the winter–spring crop earlier** (in many areas from around October–November) so that most rice was harvested before salinity peaked in February–April.
3. Provinces **closed sluice gates** and built temporary dams to protect fresh water in internal canals.
4. Farmers stored fresh water in canals, ponds and tanks before the peak.

## Lessons and actions for farmers

### Before the dry season (October–December)
1. Follow the provincial and national salinity forecasts; they are issued from late in the rainy season.
2. **Sow early** where forecasts show high salinity risk, and use short-duration varieties (about 90–105 days) so harvest is complete before about February–March.
3. Where early sowing is not possible, **leave the field fallow** or switch to a less water-demanding crop rather than risk total loss.
4. **Store fresh water** in field trenches, ponds and on-farm canals while river water is still fresh.

### During the dry season (January–May)
1. **Measure** canal water salinity before every irrigation. Do not pump if salinity is above about 1 g/L (roughly 1.5–1.6 dS/m) for young rice.
2. Coordinate with the commune and irrigation company on sluice gate closure and opening.
3. Use stored fresh water for flowering, the most sensitive stage.
4. For fruit trees (durian, pomelo, rambutan), stop irrigating with saline water, mulch the root zone, and use stored fresh water sparingly.

### After the dry season
1. **Flush** salts from fields with the first rains, draining 2–3 times before planting.
2. Check soil EC before sowing the summer–autumn crop.

## Changing farming systems

Where salinity comes every year, many farmers in coastal provinces (Ca Mau, Bac Lieu, Soc Trang, Kien Giang, Ben Tre) have moved to:
- **Rice–shrimp rotation**: shrimp in the dry, saline season and rice in the rainy season.
- Salt-tolerant and short-duration rice varieties.
- Fruit orchards with raised beds and storage ponds.

Provincial plans under the Mekong Delta climate adaptation policy encourage these shifts in saline zones and protect fresh-water zones for rice.

## Key message

Salinity intrusion in the Mekong Delta can be forecast weeks to months in advance. Early sowing, fresh water storage and coordinated sluice operation cut losses sharply between 2016 and 2020.
