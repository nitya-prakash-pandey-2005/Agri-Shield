# Alternate wetting and drying (AWD) in irrigated rice

## What AWD is

AWD is a water-saving practice for irrigated lowland rice. Instead of keeping the field flooded all the time, the farmer lets the water level drop below the soil surface and then re-floods. Done correctly ("safe AWD"), it saves water and pumping costs without reducing yield.

- Water savings of about **15–30%** of irrigation water are commonly reported.
- It also reduces methane emissions from paddy fields substantially, often by around a third or more.
- It is widely promoted in Bangladesh (boro season), the Philippines, Vietnam and Indonesia.

## Making a field water tube

1. Use a plastic pipe or bamboo tube about **25–30 cm long** and **10–15 cm diameter**.
2. Drill holes of about 0.5 cm, about 2 cm apart, over the bottom 20 cm of the tube.
3. **Install** the tube vertically so **20 cm is below the soil surface** and 5–10 cm sticks out above.
4. Remove the soil from inside the tube so you can see the water level.
5. Place the tube in a representative spot at least 1 m from the bund.

## Safe AWD steps

1. Keep the field flooded (about 2–5 cm) for the first **1–2 weeks after transplanting** to control weeds and help seedlings establish.
2. Then stop irrigating and let the water level fall.
3. When the water level in the tube is **15 cm below the soil surface**, **irrigate** again to about **5 cm** of standing water.
4. Repeat the cycle. Depending on soil and weather, a cycle takes about 3–7 days.
5. **Keep the field flooded from about one week before flowering to one week after flowering** (heading and flowering are very sensitive to water stress).
6. After flowering, resume AWD during grain filling, and **drain** the field about 1–2 weeks before harvest.

## Fertilizer with AWD

- **Apply** urea top-dress on dry or saturated soil just before re-flooding, so the water carries the nitrogen into the soil.
- Continue normal split doses of nitrogen.

## When not to use AWD, or use with care

- **Saline soils and saline water:** letting the soil dry can pull salt to the surface and concentrate salts in the root zone. In coastal areas with soil ECe above about 4 dS/m, or where irrigation water is saline, keep fields flooded with the freshest water available instead, or only use mild AWD.
- **Light sandy soils** with very fast drainage: the water level can drop quickly; check the tube daily.
- **Fields with poor water control:** where irrigation is unreliable, the farmer may not be able to re-flood in time.
- **Rainfed fields:** AWD is designed for irrigated fields.

## Benefits for farmers

- Lower pumping or water fees (fewer irrigations, often 3–5 fewer in the boro season).
- Stronger roots and less lodging.
- Less favourable conditions for some diseases and golden apple snails.
