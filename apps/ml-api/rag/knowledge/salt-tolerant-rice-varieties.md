# Salt-tolerant rice varieties by country

## How much tolerance to expect

Ordinary rice starts to lose yield at soil ECe of about 3 dS/m and loses about 12% of yield for every 1 dS/m above that. Salt-tolerant varieties survive and yield reasonably at higher levels, often about 6–8 dS/m during most of the season and higher at the seedling stage, depending on variety. No rice variety tolerates sea water (around 45–55 dS/m). Tolerant varieties work best combined with good water management: flushing, timely planting and avoiding saline irrigation at flowering.

## Bangladesh (BRRI and BINA)

**Boro (dry season, highest salinity risk in March–May):**
- BRRI dhan 47
- BRRI dhan 61
- BRRI dhan 67
- BRRI dhan 97 and BRRI dhan 99
- BINA dhan 8 and BINA dhan 10

**Aman (wet season):**
- BRRI dhan 53, BRRI dhan 54 and BRRI dhan 73 for moderately saline coastal lands
- BRRI dhan 78 has been released as tolerant of both salinity and short-term submergence

Ask the Upazila Agriculture Office (DAE) or the BRRI regional station (for example Satkhira) which varieties perform best locally.

## India (Odisha coast and other coastal areas)

- ICAR-NRRI (Cuttack) coastal saline varieties: Luna Suvarna, Luna Sampad, Luna Barial, Luna Shankhi, and the older Lunishree.
- ICAR-CSSRI CSR series: for example CSR 10, CSR 27, CSR 30 and CSR 36, mainly for saline–sodic soils.
- Traditional tolerant landraces are also used in some coastal pockets.

Odisha State Seeds Corporation and OUAT Krishi Vigyan Kendras in coastal districts can advise on seed availability.

## Vietnam (Mekong Delta)

- CLRRI OM-series varieties such as OM5451 and OM6976 have been widely used in salinity-affected provinces; their tolerance is moderate, and their main advantage is short duration so the crop finishes before peak salinity.
- ST-series aromatic varieties from Soc Trang (such as ST24 and ST25) are commonly grown in rice–shrimp systems where some salt remains.
- Traditional varieties such as Mot Bui Do are also grown in rice–shrimp fields.

Confirm current recommendations with the provincial Department of Agriculture or the local extension station, because recommended lists change each season.

## Philippines

- The PhilRice/IRRI **"Salinas"** series of NSIC-registered varieties is bred for saline coastal areas.
- Ask the Municipal Agriculturist Office or PhilRice for the Salinas lines distributed in your province.

## Indonesia (Java north coast)

- **Inpari 34 Salin Agritan** and **Inpari 35 Salin Agritan** were released as salt-tolerant irrigated varieties.
- **Inpara** varieties (Inpara 1 to Inpara 8 and later) are for swamp and tidal lowlands and tolerate some iron toxicity and salinity.
- Contact the local BPP (extension centre) or BPTP for seed.

## Practical steps

1. **Measure** soil and water EC before choosing a variety.
2. Use tolerant varieties where ECe is about 4–8 dS/m; below 4, ordinary varieties may be fine with flushing.
3. **Transplant** older seedlings (about 30–35 days), which tolerate salinity better than young ones.
4. Plant 3 seedlings per hill and use closer spacing.
5. **Apply** extra potassium and zinc (for example zinc sulphate about 10 kg/ha) on saline soils.
6. Time planting so flowering avoids the peak-salinity months.
