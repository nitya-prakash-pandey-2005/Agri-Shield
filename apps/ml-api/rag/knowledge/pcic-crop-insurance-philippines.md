# PCIC crop insurance for Philippine farmers

## What PCIC covers

The Philippine Crop Insurance Corporation (PCIC), attached to the Department of Agriculture, insures:

- **Rice** and **corn** against natural disasters (typhoon, flood, drought, earthquake, volcanic eruption) and, under multi-risk cover, pest and disease damage.
- **High-value crops** such as vegetables, sugarcane, banana, coconut and others.
- **Livestock**, fisheries, agricultural assets and farmers' life and accident cover under separate lines.

For subsistence and smallholder farmers registered in the RSBSA, the government pays the premium for rice and corn insurance under its free insurance programme, subject to available funds. Rules and coverage amounts change, so confirm details with PCIC or your Municipal Agriculturist Office (MAO).

## Who can enrol

1. **Register in RSBSA** (Registry System for Basic Sectors in Agriculture) through your MAO. RSBSA registration is required for government-subsidised insurance and most DA support.
2. Farmers who are tillers, owners, tenants or lessees can apply.
3. Enrol **before planting or within the enrolment window** each season, usually through the MAO, farmers' cooperative, irrigators' association, or PCIC field staff.

## Documents needed for enrolment

- RSBSA registration or ID
- Application form with farm location, area, crop, variety and planting date
- Proof of land tilling (certificate of land ownership, lease, or barangay certification where required)
- Valid ID

## When a loss happens — filing a claim

1. **File a Notice of Loss (NL)** as soon as possible after the damage. PCIC generally requires the notice within about **10 days** from the occurrence of loss for rice and corn. For total loss or near harvest, notify even sooner. Check the time limit printed on your policy.
2. Submit the NL to the PCIC regional office, through the MAO, or through the PCIC adjuster assigned to your area.
3. **Do not remove or replant the damaged crop** until the PCIC adjuster has inspected it, unless PCIC allows it in writing. Keep a sample area untouched if replanting is urgent.
4. Take **dated photos** of the flooded or damaged field from several points, including landmarks.
5. Keep records: seed and fertilizer receipts, planting date, variety, and any PAGASA bulletins for the event.
6. The adjuster assesses the damage percentage; payment is based on the level of damage and the amount of cover.

## Tips for farmers

- Enrol every season; cover is per cropping season.
- Keep a copy of your Certificate of Insurance Cover.
- Report losses in writing and keep the receiving copy.
- Join group enrolment through cooperatives to save time.
- Farmers in flood-prone Central Luzon should enrol before the wet season, since claims are most common from August to November.

## Related support after disasters

After major typhoons the DA may provide free seed, fertilizer and quick-response funds. RSBSA registration and PCIC records help you access these programmes quickly.
