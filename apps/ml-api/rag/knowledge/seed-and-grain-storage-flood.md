# Protecting seed and grain stocks from floods

## Why seed storage matters

After a flood, the biggest delay in replanting is often lack of good seed. Farmers who keep their own seed dry and safe can re-sow within days. Grain that gets wet starts to mould and heat within 24–48 hours and quickly becomes unfit to eat or sell.

## Moisture targets

- **Food grain (rice, maize):** dry to about **14% moisture** or lower before storage.
- **Seed:** dry to about **12% moisture** or lower. For storage longer than 6 months, aim for 10–12%.
- Grain stored above about 14% moisture heats, moulds and attracts insects.

Dry grain in thin layers (2–4 cm) on tarpaulins or nets, turning often. Do not dry seed on hot metal or tarmac at midday, because very high temperatures reduce germination.

## Hermetic storage

Hermetic (airtight) bags such as the **IRRI Super Bag** and similar multi-layer plastic liners stop air and moisture moving in or out.

1. Put the hermetic liner inside an ordinary woven sack for strength.
2. Fill with grain or seed already dried to the target moisture. Hermetic bags do not dry grain; they only keep it dry.
3. Press out air, twist the neck tightly and tie it firmly. Tie the outer sack separately.
4. Insects inside use up the oxygen and die, usually within a few weeks, without chemicals.
5. Well-dried seed in hermetic bags can keep good germination for about 9–12 months, much longer than in ordinary sacks.
6. Hermetic bags are also water-resistant for short periods, but they are not designed to sit under flood water. Always raise them.

Sealed plastic drums, metal bins and thick plastic jerry cans with tight lids are also good for small seed lots.

## Store above flood level

1. **Store** seed and grain on raised platforms, shelves or lofts at least **30 cm above the highest flood level** remembered in your village.
2. Use a raised plinth, the house loft (macha), or a community flood shelter or killa.
3. Place bags on wooden pallets, never directly on the floor or against walls, which can be damp.
4. Keep a small seed reserve (enough for at least one re-sowing) in a separate place, for example with a relative in a higher village.
5. Cover stacks with plastic sheets if the roof may leak during a cyclone.

## Before a flood warning

- Move all seed and grain to the highest safe place within 24–48 hours of a warning.
- Label bags with variety name and harvest date.
- Check that bags are tightly sealed.

## After a flood

1. Check every bag. Separate wet bags immediately.
2. Grain wet for less than about 24 hours can sometimes be saved by drying at once in thin layers.
3. **Do not use wet, mouldy or sprouted grain for food or feed**; moulds can produce harmful toxins.
4. **Test germination** of stored seed before sowing: put 100 seeds on a wet cloth for 5–7 days. If fewer than about 80 sprout, increase the seed rate or get new seed.

## Community seed banks

Village seed banks run by farmer groups, NGOs or local agriculture offices keep stocks of flood-tolerant (Sub1), salt-tolerant and short-duration varieties for emergency replanting. Register with your local group before the flood season.
