# Submergence-tolerant rice (Sub1 varieties)

## What Sub1 rice does

Sub1 varieties carry the SUB1A gene, first found in the Indian landrace FR13A. When the whole plant is under water, the gene makes the plant "wait": it stops wasting energy on stem elongation and saves its carbohydrate reserves. When the water goes down, the plant recovers and keeps growing.

- Normal high-yielding varieties usually die after about 4–7 days of complete submergence.
- Sub1 varieties typically survive about 14–17 days of complete submergence.
- Survival is best in clear, cool, flowing water. Survival is lower in turbid, hot (above about 30 °C), stagnant water with little oxygen.
- Sub1 does not help with deep stagnant water that stays for months. For that, deepwater or floating rice is needed.

Sub1 versions yield about the same as the parent variety when there is no flood, so farmers lose nothing by planting them in flood-prone fields.

## Recognised Sub1 varieties by country

- **Bangladesh:** BRRI dhan 51 (Swarna-Sub1 background) and BRRI dhan 52 (BR11-Sub1) for aman season; BINA dhan 11 and BINA dhan 12 are also released as submergence tolerant.
- **India (Odisha and eastern states):** Swarna-Sub1, Samba Mahsuri-Sub1, CR 1009 Sub1, IR64-Sub1.
- **Philippines:** NSIC Rc 194 (Submarino 1).
- **Indonesia:** Inpari 29 Rendaman and Inpari 30 Ciherang Sub1.
- **Vietnam:** Sub1 lines have been tested with CLRRI; ask the provincial agriculture department which lines are available locally.

## Nursery management

Strong seedlings survive floods much better than weak ones.

1. Use a low seed rate in the nursery, about 25–40 g of seed per square metre, so seedlings are sturdy.
2. Apply a small amount of phosphorus and well-rotted manure to the seedbed.
3. Transplant seedlings about 25–30 days old. Older, sturdier seedlings tolerate submergence better than very young ones.
4. Transplant 2–3 seedlings per hill at about 20 x 15 cm spacing.
5. Keep a reserve seedbed on high ground in case gap filling is needed.

## Fertilizer before and during the flood season

- Apply full phosphorus and potassium as basal at final land preparation.
- Split nitrogen. Do not top-dress urea just before an expected flood, because the nitrogen is lost and lush plants rot more easily under water.
- Plants with high tissue nitrogen at the time of flooding die faster.

## After the water recedes

1. **Drain gradually** over 1–2 days. Sudden exposure to strong sun after submergence can damage leaves.
2. Remove silt and debris from plants by hand where possible.
3. Wait about 7–10 days after the water recedes, until new leaves appear, then **apply about 20–30 kg N/ha** (roughly 45–65 kg urea/ha) to speed recovery.
4. If more than about 30% of hills are dead, gap-fill with seedlings from a community seedbed or the same variety.
5. Scout for bacterial leaf blight and brown spot for 2–3 weeks after the flood.

## Key numbers to remember

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Survival, Sub1 varieties | about 14–17 days complete submergence |
| Survival, normal varieties | about 4–7 days |
| Seedling age at transplanting | about 25–30 days |
| Nursery seed rate | about 25–40 g/m2 |
| Post-flood N top-dress | 20–30 kg N/ha, 7–10 days after water recedes |

## When to use Sub1 rice

Plant Sub1 varieties in low-lying fields that flood for one to two weeks in most years during the monsoon (aman, kharif, wet season). Combine them with good drainage, a reserve seedbed and crop insurance for best protection.
