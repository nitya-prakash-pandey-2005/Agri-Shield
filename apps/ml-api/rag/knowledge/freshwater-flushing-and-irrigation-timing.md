# Freshwater flushing and timing irrigation in saline areas

## The main rule

In coastal areas, canal and river salinity changes with the tide, the season and upstream river flow. **Always measure the EC of canal water before pumping it into rice fields.** For rice, irrigate only when canal water is below about **2 dS/m** (roughly 1.3 g/L). Salt-tolerant varieties can manage somewhat more, but avoid water above about 4 dS/m, especially at flowering.

Vietnamese advisories often give salinity in grams per litre (g/L or per mille). As a rough conversion, 1 g/L is about 1.5–1.6 dS/m. Many Mekong advisories treat water above about 1 g/L as unsafe for young rice.

## Measuring before pumping

1. Take the canal water sample from the depth of the pump intake.
2. **Measure** EC with a handheld meter or use the salinity reading shared by the commune, water user group or provincial salinity bulletin.
3. Record the time and tide stage.
4. Pump only if the reading is below the safe limit. If it is close to the limit, pump for a shorter time and check again.

## Tidal timing

- In tidal rivers, salinity is usually **lowest near the end of the ebb (falling) tide and around low tide**, and highest near high tide, especially during spring tides (around full and new moon).
- In the dry season, salinity rises as river flow falls, typically peaking around March–May in Bangladesh and the Mekong Delta.
- Plan pumping for low-tide periods and during neap tides when salinity is lower. Check local readings, because timing differs by location.

## Storing fresh water

1. **Store rainwater** at the end of the monsoon in ponds, canals, ditches and field trenches.
2. Close sluice gates or temporary dams on on-farm canals once good-quality water is stored, so saline water cannot enter.
3. Deepen farm ponds before the dry season; a pond of about 10% of the farm area can supplement irrigation for a small area of dry-season crops.
4. Line or compact pond walls to reduce seepage; cover with floating plants only where evaporation is high and the pond is not used for fish.
5. Save stored water for the most sensitive stages: transplanting and flowering in rice.

## Flushing salt from fields

1. At the start of the season, **flush** fields with the first good rain or fresh canal water: fill to 5–10 cm, leave 1–2 days, **drain**, repeat 2–3 times.
2. During the season, if field water EC rises above about 4 dS/m, drain it and replace with fresher water.
3. Do not let saline fields dry and crack in the dry season; salts rise to the surface as water evaporates.

## Adjusting cropping calendar

- **Sow and transplant early** so rice flowers before salinity peaks. In the Mekong Delta, early sowing of the winter–spring crop has reduced losses in drought years.
- Use short-duration varieties (about 90–105 days) where the fresh-water window is short.
- Use alternate wetting and drying cautiously; in saline fields, letting the soil dry can concentrate salts.

## Checklist before each irrigation

- [ ] Canal EC measured and below about 2 dS/m (rice)
- [ ] Tide stage checked; pump at low tide
- [ ] Provincial or local salinity bulletin checked
- [ ] Stored fresh water reserved for flowering
- [ ] Field drains open so saline water can be flushed
