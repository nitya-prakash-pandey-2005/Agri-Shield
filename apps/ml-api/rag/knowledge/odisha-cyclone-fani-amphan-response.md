# Odisha cyclones Fani (2019) and Amphan (2020) — farm response lessons

## Cyclone Fani, May 2019

Fani was an extremely severe cyclonic storm that made landfall near Puri on 3 May 2019. Strong winds and heavy rain hit Puri, Khurda, Cuttack, Jagatsinghpur and Kendrapara districts. Odisha's early warning and mass evacuation kept loss of life low compared with earlier cyclones, but agriculture and horticulture suffered heavily:

- Coconut, cashew, mango and other fruit trees were uprooted or broken, especially in Puri and Khurda.
- Betel vine (paan) gardens were flattened.
- Summer (rabi) crops such as groundnut, green gram, summer rice and vegetables were damaged.

## Cyclone Amphan, May 2020

Amphan was a super cyclone that weakened before making landfall in West Bengal near the Sundarbans on 20 May 2020. Northern coastal Odisha districts, including Balasore, Bhadrak, Kendrapara and Jagatsinghpur, faced heavy rain, strong winds and some tidal surge. Standing summer crops, vegetables and betel vines were damaged, and some low-lying fields were inundated.

## Pre-cyclone advisories that worked

The state agriculture department and KVKs issued advisories several days ahead. Key actions:

1. **Harvest** mature crops (summer rice, groundnut, green gram, vegetables) immediately; rice at about 80% maturity.
2. **Store** harvested produce and seed in safe, raised, covered places.
3. **Drain** fields and open field channels in advance.
4. Postpone fertilizer and pesticide application.
5. **Prop or stake** banana, papaya and young fruit trees; harvest ripe coconuts and tender nuts.
6. Move livestock to safe shelters with fodder and water.

## After the cyclone

### Field crops
1. **Drain** stagnant rain water and any saline surge water as quickly as possible.
2. **Measure** soil EC in surge-affected fields. **Flush** with fresh water or wait for monsoon rains to leach salts.
3. **Apply gypsum** where soil tests show sodium build-up, and add organic manure.
4. Re-sow or plant short-duration crops (vegetables, green gram) where time allows, or prepare for early kharif with salt-tolerant rice varieties from ICAR-NRRI.

### Coconut and fruit trees
1. Remove broken fronds and fallen trees; save palms with an intact crown.
2. Apply a fungicide paste (such as Bordeaux paste) on damaged crowns to prevent bud rot.
3. **Replant** with healthy seedlings at the start of the monsoon; use recommended spacing.

### Betel vine
1. Rebuild the bareja (shade structure) quickly.
2. Cut back broken vines and re-lower healthy vines.
3. Spray a recommended fungicide against foot rot, which spreads after heavy rain.

## Government support

- The state provided agricultural input subsidy to farmers with crop loss of 33% or more, under State Disaster Response Fund norms.
- Horticulture and coconut replanting support and seed distribution were provided through the district agriculture and horticulture offices.
- Farmers insured under PMFBY could claim for localised calamities and post-harvest losses; losses had to be reported within 72 hours.

## Key lessons

1. Act on IMD cyclone warnings at the 72-hour stage.
2. **Harvest early** rather than risk total loss.
3. Keep seed stocks safe for quick replanting.
4. **Insure** crops and keep land and sowing records ready for claims.
