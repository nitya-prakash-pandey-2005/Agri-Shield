# Acting on flood early warnings — 72-hour action plan

## Using flood probability

Forecasts often give a **probability** that flooding will occur in the next 72 hours. A simple decision rule helps farmers act at the right level without wasting money on false alarms.

| Flood probability (72 h) | Level | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| below about 30% | Watch | Follow forecasts daily; check drains and stores |
| about 30–60% | **Prepare** | Clear drains, plan harvest, move seed and inputs up |
| about 60–80% | **Act** | Harvest mature crops, lower field water, move animals and equipment |
| above about 80% | **Emergency** | Complete protection, evacuate animals and people if advised |

These are practical guide levels; always follow official warnings when they are stronger.

## Prepare (about 30–60%)

1. **Clear outlets** and field drains; repair bunds.
2. Check crop maturity; book harvesters and labour for crops 70–85% mature.
3. **Store seed** and grain in sealed bags on raised platforms.
4. Postpone urea top-dressing and pesticide spraying.
5. Check livestock vaccinations and store 2–3 weeks of fodder.
6. Put documents (land records, insurance papers, registration cards) in a waterproof bag.

## Act (about 60–80%)

1. **Harvest** rice with 80–85% straw-coloured grains and all mature vegetables, fruit and fish.
2. **Lower water** in rice fields to 2–3 cm.
3. **Move** pumps, tillers, fertilizer and pesticides to high ground.
4. **Close sluice gates** or field inlets if river or tidal water is rising, in coordination with the water management group.
5. Net fish ponds and raise low dike sections.
6. Move livestock to killas, embankments or shelters.

## Emergency (above about 80%)

1. Finish harvesting only if safe; otherwise leave the field.
2. **Untie animals** that cannot be moved.
3. Secure stores and cover grain with plastic.
4. Follow evacuation orders. **People's safety comes first.**

## After the flood

1. **Drain gradually** and assess damage after the water recedes.
2. Take dated photos and **report losses** within your insurance deadline.
3. Replant with stored seed or community seedbeds.

## Who to call

### Bangladesh
- **Sub-Assistant Agriculture Officer (SAAO)** in your union and the **Upazila Agriculture Officer (UAO)**, Department of Agricultural Extension (DAE).
- Upazila Livestock Officer and Upazila Fisheries Officer.
- Union Parishad and the Upazila disaster management committee.
- Flood forecasts from FFWC (BWDB) and weather from the Bangladesh Meteorological Department.

### Vietnam
- **Commune agricultural extension officer** and the commune People's Committee.
- Commune and district Steering Committee for Natural Disaster Prevention and Control.
- Forecasts from the National Centre for Hydro-Meteorological Forecasting and provincial salinity or flood bulletins.

### Philippines
- **Municipal Agriculturist Office (MAO)** and agricultural technologist assigned to your barangay.
- Municipal Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Office (MDRRMO) and barangay officials.
- PAGASA weather and flood bulletins; PCIC for insurance.

### India (Odisha)
- **Village Agriculture Worker** or Krushi Sahayak, **Assistant Agriculture Officer** and **Block Agriculture Officer**.
- Krishi Vigyan Kendra (KVK) in your district for technical advisories.
- Gram Panchayat, block office and OSDMA; IMD for cyclone and rainfall warnings.

### Indonesia
- **Field extension officer (PPL)** at the local **BPP** (agricultural extension centre).
- Pest observer (POPT) for crop damage assessment.
- Village head, and BPBD (regional disaster management agency); BMKG for weather and tidal warnings.

## Keep a record

For each warning, note the date, forecast level, actions taken, and any losses. Good records support insurance claims, government relief, and better decisions next time.
