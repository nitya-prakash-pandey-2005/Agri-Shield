# Vegetables on raised beds, sorjan and floating gardens

## Why raise the bed

Most vegetables die after 1–2 days of waterlogging and many are sensitive to salt. Raising the growing surface above water and salt keeps vegetables alive and provides food and income during the flood and dry seasons.

## Raised beds

1. Build beds about **15–30 cm high** and about 1–1.2 m wide, with 30–50 cm drainage furrows between them.
2. In flood-prone homesteads, build beds higher than the usual flood level, or use the homestead plinth.
3. Mix well-rotted manure or compost (about 2–3 kg per square metre) into the bed.
4. Connect furrows to a drain so water leaves quickly after heavy rain.
5. **Mulch** the bed surface with rice straw or water hyacinth (about 5 cm thick) to keep moisture and reduce salt rising.

## Sorjan (surjan) system

Sorjan comes from the Philippines and Indonesia (called surjan in Java). It alternates raised beds and sunken beds in the same field.

- **Raised beds** of roughly 1–2 m wide and about 0.5–1 m high for vegetables, fruit or upland crops.
- **Sunken beds** of roughly 3–6 m wide between them, for rice or fish.
- Soil dug from the sunken beds builds the raised beds.

Steps:
1. Mark alternating strips across the field.
2. Dig soil from the sunken strips and pile it on raised strips; compact the sides.
3. Plant rice in the sunken beds in the wet season; vegetables, maize or fruit on raised beds all year.
4. In coastal areas, rain washes salts from raised beds down to the sunken beds, where they can be drained.

Sorjan is also used in coastal Bangladesh and in tidal lowlands of Java.

## Floating gardens (dhap or baira, Bangladesh)

In the wetlands of Gopalganj, Pirojpur, Barisal and nearby districts, farmers grow crops on floating rafts. FAO recognises this as an agricultural heritage system.

1. Pile **water hyacinth** and other aquatic plants into a raft about **1–2 m wide, 10–50 m long and about 0.6–1 m thick**.
2. Let it decompose for 1–2 weeks.
3. Place small balls of mud and decomposed plant matter (dowlas) on top, and sow seed in them.
4. Grow vegetable seedlings, okra, gourds, red amaranth, spinach and turmeric during the flood season.
5. Tie the raft to a bamboo pole so it does not drift.
6. After the flood, the decomposed raft is used as organic manure on fields.

## Salt-tolerant vegetable choices

Based on FAO salt tolerance data (threshold ECe in dS/m):

| Vegetable | Approx. threshold ECe | Tolerance |
|---|---|---|
| Beet (garden) | 4.0 | Moderately tolerant |
| Spinach | 2.0 | Moderately sensitive |
| Tomato | 2.5 | Moderately sensitive |
| Cucumber | 2.5 | Moderately sensitive |
| Cabbage | 1.8 | Moderately sensitive |
| Chilli and pepper | 1.5 | Moderately sensitive |
| Onion | 1.2 | Sensitive |
| Beans | 1.0 | Sensitive |

- Amaranth, Indian spinach, some gourds (bottle gourd, bitter gourd), okra and watermelon are commonly grown on coastal raised beds in Bangladesh; their tolerance is moderate.
- Grow sensitive crops like beans and onion only with fresh water and low soil salinity.

## Water and salt management

1. Irrigate with the freshest water available; use stored rain water from ponds.
2. Use **pitcher (clay pot) irrigation** or drip for small areas to save water.
3. Keep soil moist; drying concentrates salt near the surface.
4. Transplant seedlings raised in fresh-water nurseries rather than direct-sowing in saline soil.

## Flood season tips

- **Harvest** all mature vegetables before a forecast flood.
- Grow vine crops (gourds, beans) on trellises over ponds or house roofs.
- Keep seed of fast crops (red amaranth, leafy greens, 25–35 days) to sow immediately after water recedes.
